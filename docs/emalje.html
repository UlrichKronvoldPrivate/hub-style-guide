Glyph discs

The sky is the only gradient

It stays exactly as it was, a touch stronger. Nothing on it is translucent any more, so the gradient reads as a ground rather than bleeding through every surface and washing it out — which is what was happening.

Opaque, hard, one flat offset

Cards are paper white by day and spruce by night, with a 2px flat edge beneath — the enamel-sign edge. No blur, no glow, no soft grey shadow. Solid tiles get the same edge in their own colour.

Colour is a fill, not a tint

The reference's whole energy is saturated solids. Skagen, coral and mustard become fills for tiles, buttons and status; the glazes stay in the sky. The hero stat and the one that needs action are solid tiles; the rest are paper.

Objects, not decoration

The reference draws its data as things — a stopwatch, a pie, a line with dots. The glyph discs and the dotted line chart do the same job. They are flat geometry in a solid disc, one per idea.

What stayed

Schibsted Grotesk alone. The seeds. Skagen for action. The archetypes, the writing rules, the a11y floor. The chart's axis labels are still HTML.

The long shadow

It is the reference's one depth cue and it is optional here — turn it on above. My view: it works on the solid tiles and fights the paper cards. If it stays, it stays on solids only.
