Colour moved into the ground

v1 put colour on components and kept the ground white — so the pastels you asked for only ever showed up as 8px squares. Here the sky is the glazes, and the components are quiet glass over it. Voltage still lives under five percent on surfaces; the atmosphere is where the pop comes from.

Elevation by tone, not shadow

Material 3's real idea, which v1 ignored: five surface levels, each a step more opaque, no drop shadows anywhere. A dialog is simply the most opaque glass. A lit top edge does the rest.

One structure, a seed per app

Material's dynamic colour, turned into a house rule: each app picks a seed that reorders the sky. Same components, same rules, recognisably the same studio — but Foundry, Danmarkspanel and Atlas are not the same app, and now they don't look it either.

Shape got a range

4px controls and 12px plates was too little travel for glass. Controls 10, plates 20, the hero tile 28, chips full. Radius still means role — it just has more of them.

What did not move

Schibsted Grotesk alone. Skagen for action, flare once. Spruce ink with hue. The four archetypes, one scroll container, the writing rules, the a11y floor, the tells table. The dot grid stays on canvas pages, where React Flow already draws one.

What it is not

Not a purple-to-blue wallpaper: the sky is our own glazes, cold glacier to warm rye, and at night it goes aurora-green rather than violet. Not Material's pill buttons or Roboto. Not four identical cards — the stat row is still one instrument, now with four tonal cells.
